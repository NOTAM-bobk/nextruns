Max-cushion shoes used to ask you to trade stability for softness — you got the plush ride and then spent the run compensating for it. This one's geometry is the story: the rocker rolls you through the stride so decisively that the big foam stack never feels like a liability.

## The ride

Landings are quiet and forgiving, and the foam does its best work exactly where max-cushion shoes should: the back half of long runs, when your legs start negotiating. The rocker keeps the transition rolling enough that even tired-leg jogging feels forward-moving rather than trudging. What it won't do is tempo work — push the pace and the foam squishes into vagueness, with no platform to lean on. This is a long-run and easy-day specialist and makes no apology for it.

## Fit and upper

The upper strikes a rare balance: generous through the forefoot without the heel slipping. Wide-footed runners will find it accommodating; narrow-footed runners may need to cinch the laces more than usual. No hot spots after break-in, and the step-in comfort means there's essentially no adaptation period.

## Durability

The thin outsole rubber is the weak point. On rough pavement the high-wear zones will smooth noticeably within the first hundred miles. The foam itself is holding bounce well so far, but plan on replacing these at the earlier end of the normal window if you run mostly on roads.

## Who it's for

Runners logging big easy miles who want maximum protection without giving up a stable ride, and anyone rotating this as the long-run shoe in a two- or three-shoe stable. If you want one shoe that does everything, or you're buying for speedwork, look elsewhere — but as the comfort specialist in a rotation, it's one of the best-executed max-cushion shoes we've tested.

## Verdict

Soft, stable, and honest about its job. A specialist worth the price for the right slot in your rotation.
