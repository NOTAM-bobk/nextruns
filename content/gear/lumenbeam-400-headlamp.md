A headlamp lives or dies on two things: whether the light lets you actually run technical terrain at night, and whether you forget you're wearing it six hours in. This one passes both.

## Light quality

The beam is the strong point. The wide mode throws an even flood across the trail that keeps roots and rocks visible in your natural field of view, not just in a bright circle at your feet. The spot mode reaches far enough to read the next switchback on dark descents. The wide-plus-spot combination is the setting most night runners will live in, and it's well tuned — bright center, useful periphery, no harsh tunnel effect.

## Comfort

The strap tension and weight distribution are dialed: no forehead pressure point even after an all-night ultra effort, and the lamp doesn't bounce on downhill sections. For overnight events, this is the difference between forgetting the light and fighting it.

## Battery and controls

The removable rechargeable battery is a real advantage for multi-day stage events or bike-and-run missions — carry a spare and you're covered for a weekend. The downside is feedback: the single-LED gauge tells you almost nothing about remaining charge, so you're guessing on long nights. Carry the spare or a power bank and treat the gauge as decorative. The mode buttons also require precise presses that get annoying with gloves — a real consideration for winter use.

## Who it's for

Night runners, ultras, and anyone who wants one reliable light for dark-morning training through multi-day events. If you need precise battery telemetry or operate exclusively in gloves and deep cold, compare carefully. For everyone else, the beam quality and comfort justify the price on their own.

## Verdict

Excellent light, excellent comfort, mediocre interface. The fundamentals are what you're paying for, and they deliver.
