The GPS running watch market has drifted toward doing everything — sleep, stress, blood oxygen, on-wrist coaching — and it's easy for the actual running features to get lost in the feature list. This one keeps the basics sharp while still offering the extras.

## Tracking accuracy

GPS accuracy is the single most important spec for a running watch, and it's where this one performs best. Route tracking stayed tight and consistent through dense tree cover on trail runs and in the kind of urban canyons that usually cause satellite drift, with pace readings that matched known-distance test loops closely.

## Battery life

Rated for roughly 10 days of daily use with GPS tracking on for typical training runs, which held up well in testing — a full week of training plus a long run didn't require a mid-week charge, something that isn't universal in this watch class.

## Everyday usability

The button-plus-touchscreen combination mostly works well, though the touchscreen response degrades noticeably with sweaty fingers on hot runs — the physical buttons become the more reliable input at that point, which is a reasonable fallback but worth knowing going in.

Training load, recovery time, and readiness metrics are presented clearly on the watch face without requiring a deep dive into the app, though a few of the more granular training-effect breakdowns are gated behind a companion app subscription.

## Who it's for

Runners who want reliable GPS tracking and solid battery life as the priority, with useful (not overwhelming) training metrics layered on top. If you specifically want deep multi-sport features for triathlon or want every advanced metric unlocked without a subscription, it's worth comparing against higher-tier options in the same lineup.

## Verdict

A strong all-around running watch that nails the fundamentals most runners actually use daily, with enough extra depth to grow into over a training cycle.
