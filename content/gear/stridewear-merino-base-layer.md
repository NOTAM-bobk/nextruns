Winter base layers usually force a choice: synthetic layers that perform but reek, or merino that feels great but sags and pills. This one is the merino that makes the synthetic crowd nervous.

## Warmth and range

The fabric weight hits a sweet spot: warm enough to anchor a winter kit near freezing, breathable enough that it doesn't become a sauna at 10–12°C with a light shell. That breadth is the practical win — one layer covers most of a temperate winter instead of a drawer of specialized pieces. Wet-out behavior is typical merino: it absorbs rather than repels, so hard rain still wants a shell over the top.

## Odor and care

The odor resistance is real, not marketing. A week of sweaty runs without meaningful smell changes the logistics of training travel and back-to-back sessions. Washing is gentle-cycle-and-air-dry; it has held shape and softness through a full season of this so far.

## Fit and durability

The cut is athletic without compression — snug enough to layer over, relaxed enough to wear alone on shoulder-season mornings. Flat seams caused no chafing over long runs. The durability caveat is abrasion: the fine merino knit snags easily on brush, hydration vest velcro, and pack straps. Layer it under things that don't grab and it should last; treat it as a trail-bashing workhorse and it will look old fast.

## Who it's for

Winter and shoulder-season runners who value comfort across a wide temperature band and hate laundry as much as they hate being cold. The price is real but the use case is broad — this replaces several cheaper layers rather than adding to the pile.

## Verdict

A quiet, effective upgrade to any cold-weather kit. Buy it for the comfort range; baby it around velcro.
