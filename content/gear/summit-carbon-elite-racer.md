Carbon-plated racing shoes have mostly converged on a formula — stiff plate, high-stack lightweight foam, aggressive rocker — but the execution still varies a lot between models. This one lands closer to the "stable and fast" end of that spectrum than the "fast but twitchy" end.

## On the road

At marathon and half-marathon effort, the plate's propulsive snap is genuinely noticeable, particularly on faster segments where you can lean into the rocker geometry. Unlike some highly aggressive racing shoes, it doesn't feel unstable at slower paces, which makes it more usable for tempo workouts, not just race day.

## Fit and lockdown

The upper is minimal, as expected for a racing shoe, but the midfoot lockdown is secure enough that foot slide inside the shoe isn't a concern even over a full marathon distance. Sizing runs true, though the low-volume fit will feel tight for runners with wider feet.

## Durability and use case

This isn't a shoe for daily training — the outsole rubber is intentionally minimal to save weight, and it will wear quickly under regular mileage. Reserve it for race day and select workouts (marathon-pace long runs, tempo efforts) where the propulsive ride earns its keep.

## Who it's for

Runners training for a road race who want a genuine performance upgrade on race day and are willing to pay a premium price and accept limited shoe lifespan in exchange. Less suited to trail, treadmill-only training, or runners who prioritize a plush daily-training feel over race-day speed.

## Verdict

One of the more balanced carbon racers available — fast without feeling reckless underfoot. If your training and racing paces are close enough that you'd feel the difference, it's worth the investment for goal races.
