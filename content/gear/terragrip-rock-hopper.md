Most trail shoes are compromises that average out to mediocrity on every surface. This one commits: it's built for technical terrain, and on wet rock and gnarly descents it's about as sure-footed as trail shoes get.

## Grip

The lug compound and pattern are the headline. On wet slab and slick roots — the surfaces that end most trail runs early — the traction stayed planted where several better-known shoes we've tested slid. Descents on loose rock are equally confident; you stop planning your footfalls and just run, which is the real test.

## Protection and feel

The rock plate threads the needle: sharp talus stops being an event underfoot, yet the shoe retains enough ground feel for precise placements on technical ridgelines. That balance is what separates a shoe you race technical terrain in from one you merely survive it in.

## Fit

This is the main caveat. The fit runs narrow through the midfoot and toe box, snug in a way that locks the foot down on sidehills but will squeeze wide feet uncomfortably. Try before buying if your feet run wide; sizing up adds length without adding the missing width.

## Where it shouldn't go

Deep, aggressive lugs on fire roads feel clunky and wear fast on pavement. If your trail running is mostly smooth and graded, a lighter all-rounder will serve better for half the trade-offs. This shoe's price is justified by terrain difficulty, not by versatility.

## Verdict

For technical mountain running, among the best grip-and-protection combinations available. Buy it for the hard stuff, not for the canal path.
