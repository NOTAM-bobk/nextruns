Bounce is the defining problem with running vests, and it's the difference between a vest you forget you're wearing and one you're constantly adjusting. This one solves it better than most vests in its price range.

## Fit and stability

The dual-adjust chest straps and contoured shoulder panels let you dial in a genuinely snug fit without needing to crank down so hard it restricts breathing. Loaded with two soft flasks and a light layer, bounce at a moderate trail running pace was minimal — noticeably better than similarly priced competitors.

## Storage and accessibility

Two front pockets hold standard soft flasks with easy one-handed access, plus a zippered pocket for gels or a phone. The main compartment handles a light layer, extra nutrition, and a basic first aid kit comfortably; it's not built for multi-day self-supported efforts, but that's not really the category it's competing in.

## Comfort over distance

The mesh back panel breathes well and didn't cause chafing over a three-hour effort in warm conditions, which is a common failure point for budget vests. The included soft flasks are fine but not exceptional — many runners will eventually swap them for a preferred brand, which the vest accommodates without issue.

## Who it's for

Runners doing long trail runs and shorter ultras who want a stable, comfortable vest without stepping up to a $150+ price point. If you're training for a full-day or multi-day ultra requiring more mandatory gear, size up to a higher-capacity vest instead.

## Verdict

A well-executed lightweight vest that gets the most important thing — stability — right, at a price that doesn't require choosing between quality and budget.
