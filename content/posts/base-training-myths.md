Base training might be the most misunderstood phase in recreational running, mostly because it's described by what it isn't — no workouts, no racing, just "getting miles in." Here are four myths that keep producing wasted winters.

## Myth 1: Base miles are junk miles

The myth says slow running is filler between real training. The physiology says the opposite: easy volume is what raises capillary density, mitochondrial content, and fat oxidation — adaptations with no shortcut — and it's also what thickens tendons and bones against the harder work to come. A well-run base phase isn't a placeholder. It's the phase that determines how much the workout phase can hold.

## Myth 2: Strides and short hills "ruin" the base

Some runners treat any pace above easy as contamination during base season. In reality, weekly strides — 6 to 8 relaxed accelerations of about 20 seconds — and short hill surges are standard practice in almost every serious base phase ever written. They preserve leg speed and running mechanics without meaningful recovery cost. What they don't do is turn your aerobic phase into an anaerobic one. Add them; thank yourself in March.

## Myth 3: More miles always beats fewer, better miles

Aerobic development scales with volume — up to the point where your ability to absorb running breaks down. Beyond that, extra miles don't build the base, they spend it: suppressed quality for weeks, niggles that become injuries, and a logbook that looks impressive while fitness stalls. The right base volume is the most you can run *while staying genuinely fresh* — which for one runner is 30 kilometers a week and for another is 130. The number that matters is yours, not the internet's.

## Myth 4: Base means only running

Cross-training gets treated as base-season heresy by one camp and a complete substitute by another. Both are wrong in the same way. Cycling or swimming can add aerobic stimulus with less orthopedic cost — genuinely useful as a third or fourth "run" for injury-prone runners — but running economy is specific: you get economical at running by running. The sensible version is running as the base, with other aerobic work as a supplement sized to your injury history, not a replacement.

## What a real base week looks like

Almost everything easy — truly easy, by heart rate or talk test. One slightly longer run. Strides twice a week. Volume that repeats comfortably week to week, with a modest step-back every third or fourth week. No heroics, no GPS screenshots of paces you're slightly embarrassed by. Boring, repeatable, and quietly building the engine that everything else this year will run on.
