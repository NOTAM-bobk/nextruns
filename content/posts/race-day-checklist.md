The best thing you can do the night before a race is remove every decision you can from race morning. Here's the list.

## Lay out, don't pack

Lay your full race kit out on the floor in the order you'll put it on: socks, shorts or tights, top, bib pinned on, shoes with laces already tied to your usual tension, watch charged, and any gels or fuel already in your pockets. If you're driving or taking transit to the start, lay out that bag too — throwaway layer, post-race change of clothes, any medical or ID information.

## Pin your bib now, not in the morning

This sounds trivial and it isn't — fumbling with safety pins in the dark, half-awake, is a genuinely bad use of race-morning adrenaline. Pin it the night before, and double check the timing chip (if separate) is attached where it needs to be.

## Sort your fueling plan into the actual order you'll eat it

If your plan is a gel at 45 minutes, another at 90, and sports drink at aid stations from mile 10 onward, lay the gels out in that order, not just loose in a pocket. On tired legs at mile 18, you don't want to be reading tiny gel labels to figure out which one you already used.

## Set two alarms, and do the math backward

Work backward from your start time: travel time plus buffer, plus time to eat breakfast 2–3 hours before the gun, plus time to use the bathroom more than once, plus a genuine buffer for anything going wrong. Then set a backup alarm 10 minutes after the first. Race mornings are a bad time to discover your phone silently updated its clock or your alarm didn't go off.

## Write down your plan, briefly

A few lines is enough: goal pace or effort for each segment, your fueling schedule, and one or two reminders for yourself — "start conservative," "smile through the middle miles," whatever tends to go sideways for you specifically. Reading it again in the morning takes thirty seconds and offloads things you don't want to be figuring out live at mile 20.

## What not to do

- Don't try a new breakfast, new shoes, or new gear tonight or tomorrow morning.
- Don't do a hard workout "just to feel ready" — the fitness is already banked.
- Don't stay up scrolling race-day anxiety threads. Read something boring and go to sleep.

Nothing on this list is exciting, and that's exactly the point — the night before a race should be as uneventful as possible, so the only thing left to do in the morning is show up and run.
