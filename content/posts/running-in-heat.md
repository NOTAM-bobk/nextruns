The first hot run of the year always feels like a fitness collapse. It isn't. Performance degrades measurably at temperatures well below the point where you feel miserable — pace at a given effort starts sliding somewhere around 15°C and keeps sliding from there. Understanding why is most of the battle.

## The blood-shunting problem

Your muscles and your skin are competing for the same blood. When you run in heat, a large share of your cardiac output gets diverted to the skin for cooling, which means less oxygen delivery per heartbeat — your heart rate climbs at any given pace, and your pace at any given effort drops. Add sweat-driven fluid loss and the picture compounds: as plasma volume shrinks, each heartbeat moves less blood, and the drift accelerates. This is why heart-rate-guided pacing on hot days is a sanity device — if you hold the same heart rate, you'll naturally run slower and finish functional.

## Acclimatization is real and it takes about two weeks

Heat adaptation is one of the most reliable adaptations in exercise physiology: after 10–14 days of regular heat exposure, plasma volume expands, you start sweating earlier and more dilutely, and the same pace costs measurably less. The protocol is unglamorous — run easy in the heat, or add 20–30 minutes of post-run heat exposure (including a hot bath, if you're committed), keep intensity down, and don't judge fitness by hot-weather paces. A heat-adapted runner racing in cool conditions gets a genuine performance bump, which is why some athletes train for fall races through August deliberately.

## Practical rules that survive contact with a heatwave

- **Shift, don't shrink.** Early morning beats evening for most summer days, and shade-heavy routes beat open ones. Move the long run to the coolest window rather than cutting it.
- **Pre-hydrate, then drink to thirst.** Starting a long run well-hydrated matters more than anything you drink during the first hour. Beyond that, drink to thirst on runs under 90 minutes; add sodium on longer or sweatier efforts.
- **Dress for evaporation.** Light-colored, loose, minimal. If you're wearing it and it's not protecting you from sun, it's trapping heat.
- **Rebuild the easy days.** Heat turns yesterday's easy pace into today's moderate effort. Slow down more than feels reasonable, and cut the workout if quality becomes fiction.
- **Know the red flags.** Confusion, chills, or a sudden stop in sweating are not "pushing through" territory. Heat illness escalates fast and the finish line is never worth it.

## The one upside

Every hard summer makes autumn feel like flying. The fitness you maintained through the heat, plus the 3–6% performance bonus of cool air, is why September races produce so many breakthroughs. Survive the summer with patience and you arrive at your goal race with an edge most runners throw away by fighting July.
