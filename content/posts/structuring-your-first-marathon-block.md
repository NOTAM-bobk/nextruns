Most first-time marathoners overcomplicate the plan and undertrain the one run that matters most. Here's a simpler way to think about a 16-week block.

## The three phases

**Base (weeks 1–6).** The goal here isn't fitness, it's durability. You're teaching your legs, tendons, and connective tissue to tolerate volume before you ask them to tolerate speed. Keep almost everything easy. If you're not sure whether a run is easy enough, it isn't — slow down until you could hold a conversation without gasping.

**Build (weeks 7–12).** This is where the marathon-specific work happens: your long run grows toward 18–20 miles, and you introduce one weekly quality session — a tempo run or some marathon-pace miles — without touching the rest of your easy running. Resist adding a second hard day. One is enough to drive adaptation; two is usually enough to drive injury.

**Taper (weeks 13–16).** Volume drops by roughly 40–60% over the final three weeks while intensity stays. This is the phase runners sabotage most often, convinced that cutting mileage means losing fitness. It doesn't — you're not losing fitness in three weeks, you're letting the last three months of it show up on race day.

## The long run is the whole point

If you only have time to protect one run a week, protect the long run. It's the single best predictor of how your race will go, because it's the only run that rehearses the actual demand of the marathon: hours on your feet, glycogen depletion, and the mental fatigue of the back half.

Grow it by no more than 10% a week, and take a step-back week every third or fourth week where you drop the long run by a few miles to let your body absorb the work. A long run that leaves you wrecked for three days isn't building fitness faster — it's just borrowing from next week's training.

## What to skip

- **Don't chase a weekly mileage number you saw online.** The right volume is whatever you can absorb and recover from, not whatever a training plan template says a "real" marathoner runs.
- **Don't do speedwork you don't have a reason for.** Track intervals build a different kind of fitness than the marathon asks for. Tempo and marathon-pace work carry more transfer for most first-timers.
- **Don't treat the taper as optional.** The fitness is already banked. The taper is what lets you use it.

## A rough week, mid-build

- Monday: rest or easy 30–40 min
- Tuesday: easy 45–60 min
- Wednesday: quality session (tempo or marathon-pace miles)
- Thursday: easy 40–50 min
- Friday: rest or short shakeout
- Saturday: easy 30–40 min
- Sunday: long run

That's it. No plan survives contact with real life perfectly — sickness, work travel, and bad weeks happen. What matters is the shape: patient base, one hard day, a long run you respect, and a taper you trust.
